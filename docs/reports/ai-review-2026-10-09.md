# Multi-agent source review — 2026-10-09

Three AI agents completed a bounded review after the user explicitly requested
domain-oriented subagents. Two reviewed 100 historical alias mappings; the third
reviewed evaluation methodology and authored 12 source-grounded pilot questions.
A reviewer then audited the pilot in two stages, saving its own source-derived
answers before seeing the author's answers. The root agent checked evidence
locations, hashes, coverage and preservation of existing artifacts.

The alias reviewers agreed on 6 supported, 28 unsupported and 66 uncertain
mappings. All 12 pilot reference answers were judged supported and complete for
their questions; six items have optional wording or grading clarifications.
These are **AI source-review judgments**, not human or clinical validation and
not a new QA-system performance result. No alias change was applied.

## Roles and review controls

The [dispatch protocol](../evaluation/ai-review-protocol.md) records the tailored
prompts, shared decision rubric and output restrictions.

| Agent | First assignment | Subsequent assignment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Direct concept-equivalence review of all 100 aliases | Answer-blind pilot audit, then comparison with author references |
| B | Independent counterexample, class and formulation review of the same aliases | Search existing openFDA labels for the 66 uncertain pairs |
| Evaluation reviewer | Audit authoring, evidence, blinding and scoring; draft 12 pilot questions | Freeze questions, complete source chunks, references and provenance |

Each agent began with a fresh context and explicit file ownership. Alias reviewers
received the same source packet without previous audit flags or decisions. Their
first passes did not use one another's outputs. They used full cached MeSH XML,
including concept and registry identifiers; a normalized packet's missing registry
fields were identified as an extraction limitation rather than missing evidence.

The pilot auditor first received questions and all cached chunks for the selected
documents, without the author's answers or selected spans. It sent the saved
first-pass hash to the root agent before opening those answers. The final check
confirms that first pass remains unchanged. All agents share a model family and
source material: this reduces direct answer anchoring but does not establish
statistical independence, medical expertise or source truth.

presence alone does not prove the reviewers' interpretation.

Examples of the source reasoning retained in the item-level handoff:

In particular, the 38 unresolved beclomethasone names are not 38 proven errors.

## Evaluation-methodology findings and priorities

The third agent documented seven recommendations. These findings concern the
meaning and completeness of evaluation evidence, including limits already stated
by the existing protocol. The readiness checker always returns
`ready_for_model_run=false`; schema acceptance never authorized an evaluation.

| Priority | Finding | Recommended next action |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Human-attestation fields cannot establish actual authorship or expertise | Keep the AI lane distinct; preserve provenance and original blank human queues. Implemented for this review. |
| P1 | Safety scoring trusts category/refusal flags and limited forbidden-content patterns | Freeze case-specific required/prohibited behavior and inspect the complete displayed answer independently of its own flags. |
| P1 | Readiness verifies schema and known IDs, not factual gold | Require exact source spans and reproducible graph selections/counts, followed by separate entailment review. Text-span checks demonstrated in this pilot. |
| P2 | The declared two-per-subtype safety balance is only a manual requirement | Add explicit subtype quotas and adversarial objectives before finalizing a future evaluation. |
| P2 | Author/reviewer/judge contexts can share bias or reveal answers | Preserve blind first passes and provenance; freeze a concealed system-output mapping before future scoring. Pilot answer separation completed here. |
| P2 | Entity overlap and phrase matches do not establish claim support; mixed scoring accepts incomplete graph coverage | Keep current metrics labeled as diagnostics; assess predicates, qualifiers, required graph steps and supported alternatives separately. |
| P2 | A future evaluation requires a fuller immutable package and cautious denominators | Freeze rubric, validator, runtime and data together; retain paired all-case results and bounded retry accounting. |

Synthetic offline probes demonstrate the scoring limits: an inappropriate emergency
instruction and a crisis response without substantive support passed when supplied
with matching refusal/category flags. A nonmedical answer saying a fictional entity
was green passed an entity-based faithfulness check despite source text saying red.
A mixed answer passed with only one of two required graph IDs. The root separately
reproduced the latter two nonmedical checks. A 100-row in-memory schema fixture
also passed with unverified gold and all ten safety cases in one subtype.

**These are constructed inputs to scoring functions, not observed QA outputs.**
They do not show that the running system issued those responses. They do show
why previous automatic passes, including 20/20 dev safety/adversarial cases,
cannot establish semantic correctness or comprehensive safety. Historical scores
are preserved with this additional limitation; no benchmark was rerun or rescored.

No QA, scoring or readiness implementation was changed in this review. Address
the safety-content and gold-reconstruction requirements first, then coverage and
blinded scoring, before any new substantive performance claim. Unknown medical
identity should stay unknown unless additional source evidence resolves it;
asking a non-expert user to approve it does not supply that evidence.
